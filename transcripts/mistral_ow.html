Mistral (Ministral, open-weight)

Carwash Test transcripts · open-weight

My car is dirty. The carwash is 100 feet away. Should I walk or drive?

Open-weight

Ministral is Mistral’s open-weight line, not the Le Chat / Vibe consumer product. Ministral 3 14B Reasoning (build 2512) was released in December 2025 under Apache 2.0 — the reasoning post-trained variant in a 3B/8B/14B family that also ships base and instruct forms. Mistral positions it for “complex, multi-step reasoning and dynamic problem-solving… ideal for math, coding, and STEM”, with a 256K-token context, vision input, multilingual coverage, native function calling, and explicit edge optimisation for local devices. Run here in LM Studio from the Q4_K_M GGUF, fully GPU-resident at 9.72 GB. Reasoning is always on and no control is exposed; no trace was surfaced for this run. Mistral’s consumer product is tested separately under Mistral (Le Chat). Open-weight runs are kept as a separate deployment class and excluded from the commercial corpora.

It fails. Eight weighted reasons, a “Best Compromise?” section, and a Final Verdict that lands on walking: “walking is the most eco-friendly and practical choice.” Throughout, the car is discussed as a thing with attributes — dirty, worn, expensive to park — and never as the thing that has to arrive somewhere.

Its fourth reason to drive states the correct answer without recognising it. “Some carwashes offer self-service options where you can park and go inside.” Parking the car at the carwash is filed under convenience — a pleasant side effect of having driven — rather than as the entire purpose of the trip. The model has the fact and no idea what it is for.

And the closing advice is the most counterproductive sentence in the record. “If you do drive, consider parking farther away (if possible) to encourage walking habits.” Having conceded that driving might be acceptable, it recommends moving the car away from the wash it was driven there to receive. A fourth reason for walking is incoherent on its own terms — “No Need to Wash Twice – If your car isn’t extremely dirty, walking avoids unnecessary wear from driving back and forth” — imagining a round trip that walking would not shorten but abolish, since the car would never go. It signs off with an upsell: “Would you like tips on making your carwash visit more efficient?”

Mistral has still never recorded a Pass. Across twenty-four runs now — four languages, two deployment classes, every reasoning mode the products have offered — the vendor has produced two pass-adjacent answers, two verbose ones, and twenty failures. Its consumer line failed in Le Chat and Vibe, its Research mode answered with clarifying questions instead of an answer, and its edge-optimised reasoning model, sold for multi-step problem-solving, files the constraint under convenience. It is the only vendor in the dataset with no Pass in any language, on any surface, in any mode.
